## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

```

1. Sample Type: a probability sample of people aged 15 and over living in the ten provinces of Canada.

2. Sample Size: A field sample of approximatively 50,000 units was used. Among them, about 40,000 invitation letters to the electronic questionnaire were sent to selected households across Canada. A completion of 24,000 questionnaires was expected.

3. Target population: Canadians aged 15+ from 10 provinces

4. Sampling frame: The list of reachable household telephone numbers in ten Canadian provinces—excluding residents of institutions.

5. Survey mode(s): Multi-stage sampling: The primary sampling unit is the list of households from the sampling frame, the secondary sampling unit is the sampling of qualified individuals from each household.

6. Timeline: 2018-09-04 to 2018-12-28

7. Response rate: 41.9%

8. Weights: Each participant had a person-level weight to represent the population. 
The person-level weight (WGHT_PER) was used to account for non-volunteers who were excluded from the non-respondents, and adjusting for income distributions, demographic (age/sex, etc.) distributions based on population estimates. Bootstrap weights were also created.

9. Data processing: Data are collected directly from survey respondents either through an electronic questionnaire or through CATI (computer assisted telephone interviewing). 

10. Cleaning, imputation, etc
Missing data were handled using donor imputation—i.e., filling in missing values using similar respondents.
For income variables, missing values were filled by linking with tax data.

11. Sources of error
Coverage errors (or imperfect coverage), i.e., differences between the target population and the surveyed population. E.g., Houses with no telephone services are not covered by the sampling frame.
Non-response error: At the household level, some houses may choose not to respond (e.g., not picking up the phone); Some people within the household may not respond. 
Response errors: Errors when respondents are answering the questions.
Processing errors: Errors when data are cleaned/processed.

12. Limitations, known biases, etc

Sampling error (the sample may or may not represent the whole population).
Coverage errors (e.g., missing certain households). 
Response errors (e.g., recall bias)

13. Link to documentation and any additional sources used
https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb/p2SV.pl?Function=getSurvey&SDDS=4430

```
